About me

Chinese is where your story lands — I make it land beautifully.

I grew up between two languages in Chengdu, studied translation in Paris, and have spent the last six years doing the same thing over and over: lifting meaning out of one language and rebuilding it in Chinese until it no longer reads like a translation.

My clients are publishers, law firms, brand teams and film studios. That means long novels that need a voice, contracts where one wrong character costs real money, and campaigns where rhythm matters as much as meaning.

Questions

Asked every week

If yours isn't here, write to me in English, French, Spanish or Chinese and I'll answer within a day.

Do you use machine translation?

Never for the text you receive. I may run a passage through MT to see how a sentence could be structured, then write the Chinese myself. Light post-editing at a lower rate is a different service — plenty of people offer it, and I don't.

How fast can you turn something around?

3,000–4,000 words per working day. A 2,000-word certificate or press release can be back within 24 hours; a 90,000-word novel needs six to eight weeks of real calendar time, including the review pass.

Can you issue certified or sworn translations?

Yes. I'm registered as a sworn translator for German and French into Chinese and can provide a signed statement, letterhead and stamp-ready layout for consulates, universities and courts. Add 24–48 hours and a small certification fee.

How is confidentiality handled?

I sign NDAs without drama and can work inside your own secure platform. Client text is never pasted into public AI tools; where machine-aided concordance is useful, it runs offline on an encrypted disk.

What about payment and revisions?

50% to book the slot and 50% on delivery; publishers and agencies can ask for 30 days net. Two revision rounds are included within 30 days of delivery — and any error of mine is corrected free of charge, whenever you find it.
